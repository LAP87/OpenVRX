# FPV goggles and head-mounted displays with HDMI (or DisplayPort) video input: technical fact sheet for a 720p DVI-style TMDS source (as of 2026-10-12)

> **Scope.** Goggles/HMDs that accept an external digital video input, judged against a source that sends 1280x720 @ 60 Hz (also 59.94 Hz with frames alternating 750/751 lines, and 720p50), no audio, possibly no HDMI InfoFrames.
> **Evidence labels.** **[MFR]** manufacturer page, manual or release notes. **[CODE]** open-source firmware source (HDZero). **[MEAS]** third-party measurement with a stated method. **[REVIEW]** reviewer hands-on statement. **[USER]** forum or comment report. **[RETAIL]** retailer or affiliate-blog spec text (retailer links withheld per the brief; retailer type is given instead). **(pre-2024)** marks sources older than 2024.
> **Research limits.** The web-search quota ran out partway through the session, so some planned lookups were not done (FCC internal photos, newer Fat Shark HDO+ reviews, iFlight goggles, BetaFPV VR04 HD, Swedish retailers). Vendor firmware binaries were deliberately not downloaded (see Key question 4, Gaps). Prices are as shown on 2026-10-12 unless another date is given; retailer and manufacturer-store prices are kept in separate link-free bullets by design (retailer type and date only), while prices quoted by reviewers carry the review link. Where a fact comes only from a search-engine page summary (page body not re-read), it is marked "(search summary)".

## Key question 1: Which goggles accept 1280x720 @ 60 Hz over HDMI, and which insist on 1080p?

### Takeaway
No HDMI-input goggle was found that insists on 1080p. Every HDMI-input model with a published or tested mode list takes 720p60 or a plain "60FPS" input: the HDZero family (manual, firmware code, user test), Skyzone SKY04X and Cobra X (tested through a Walksnail VRX), Skyzone SKY04O/O Pro (manual: "60FPS"), Fat Shark HDO2 (tested), and the Orqa FPV.One Pilot, which per a 2023 review accepts ONLY 720p60. 1080p is also accepted by HDZero (1080p60, documented), Skyzone SKY04X V2/V3 and, per users, SKY04X Pro (1080p60), and Walksnail Goggles X (a 1920x1080 PC signal). DJI Goggles 2/3/N3/Integra, Walksnail Goggles L, Fat Shark Recon HD and BetaFPV VR03/VR04 have no HDMI/DP input at all. No vendor documents 59.94 Hz or 750/751-line handling.

### Inferences
- 720p60 is the safest common denominator across all HDMI-input goggles found. The vendor statements ("60FPS", "720P 100FPS", "1080p60") give frame rates or sizes, not pixel clocks or total line counts, so they say nothing about 59.94 Hz or 750/751-line alternation.
- Orqa FPV.One Pilot is the strictest sink found (720p60 only per the 2023 review); treat it as the worst-case test target, and note the review predates current firmware.
- HDZero is tolerant on frame rate but strict on size: the active area must be exactly 1280x720 or the screen stays blank.

### Gaps
- No explicit 720p60 statement for SKY04X Pro (only 720p100 and 1080p60 reports), Cobra X V4, Fat Shark HDO+, Eachine EV300O/D, or Walksnail Goggles X; their mode lists are not published.
- Orqa's 720p60-only result is from Feb 2023 and was not re-checked against current Orqa firmware; Orqa's own page lists no supported modes.
- SKY04L and "Recon HD Box" HDMI status unresolved (above).

## Key question 2: Which accept 50 Hz (720p50 or 1080p50)?

### Takeaway
Only the HDZero family has documented 50 Hz HDMI-input support (720p50 and 1080p50 in firmware, BoxPro DVR table, and a 2023 user test). For Skyzone, Fat Shark, Orqa, Walksnail Goggles X, Eachine and Goovis, 50 Hz input is unknown; the Walksnail VRX most of them were validated against only emits 60/100 Hz modes.

### Cited Findings
- HDZero firmware: 720p `fps < 57` gives HDMIIN_VTMG_720P50 and 1080p `fps < 57` gives 1080P50; a received register value of 19 (CEA VIC 19 = 720p50) or 31 (VIC 31 = 1080p50) forces fps = 50. The shipped EDID lists VIC 31 (1080p50) and its first detailed timing decodes to 1920x1080 at exactly 50.000 Hz (148.5 MHz, 2640x1125). [CODE] — [it66021.c](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/blob/adb901d9493a3f3d0ca3b052eaa693e51046dd3a/src/driver/it66021.c#L221-L288); EDID bytes as removed in [commit f1b6c45](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/commit/f1b6c45490e668cde448edb3f78175c03e7bb95a) (decoded by the researcher with a small script)
- HDZero BoxPro manual lists HDMI-in DVR timings "1280x720 (50, 60, 90 fps)" and "1920x1080 (50, 60 fps)". [MFR] — [BoxPro manual](https://www.hd-zero.com/_files/ugd/967e02_a9519d079ad64e1d8e6bcf0aadace29a.pdf)
- A user verified 720p50 and 1080p50 on a patched 9.3.0 build (27 Nov 2023); the reporter's PC defaulted to 50 Hz and switching to 60 Hz is what exposed the detection bug. [USER] — [issue #374](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/issues/374)
- HDZero's own HDMI output emits "1280x720x59.97fps" for NTSC analog and "1280x720x50fps" for PAL, i.e. HDZero hardware itself produces 59.94 and 50 Hz 720p; "HDMI in" is "Not supported" for HDMI-out pass-through. [MFR] — [HDZero Goggle manual v2.1](https://www.hd-zero.com/_files/ugd/967e02_e569535d755544d4b0300dde2f13e21f.pdf)
- HDZero docs home page: "the HDZero receiver will work with any FPV goggle with an HDMI input." [MFR] — [HDZero docs](https://docs.hd-zero.com/)
- Walksnail VRX HDMI output modes are only "1080p60fps, 720p100fps, or 720p60fps"; the page mentions neither 59.94 Hz nor 50 Hz. [MFR] — [Caddx VRX page](https://www.caddxfpv.com/blogs/news/walksnail-avatar-vrx-hdmi-digital-fpv-receiver)
- A Fat Shark Scout HD screen was described as "a 50hz display" (analog goggle, comment of 20 Dec 2022, pre-2024); this is a panel refresh remark, not an HDMI-input statement. [USER] — [IntoFPV thread](https://intofpv.com/archive/index.php/thread-22188.html)

### Inferences
- 720p50 is only evidenced on HDZero. For any other goggle it must be bench-tested; a failure would most likely appear as a black screen or an "Unsupported HDMI input" style error (the Orqa behaviour reported in 2023).
- HDZero's own 59.97/50 Hz HDMI output means 59.94 Hz and 50 Hz 720p are signals HDZero hardware itself emits, and HDZero says its receiver works with any goggle that has HDMI input. That is a hint, not proof, that other goggles tolerate them.

### Gaps
- No 50 Hz evidence for Skyzone, Fat Shark HDO2/HDO+, Orqa, Walksnail Goggles X, Eachine or Goovis.
- Whether the HDZero VRX/goggle HDMI output sends AVI InfoFrames with correct VICs for 59.97 and 50 Hz was not determined (the IT66121 init in the firmware is an opaque register dump).

## Key question 3: Does any goggle accept a DVI-style signal without HDMI infoframes, or is that unknown?

### Takeaway
Unknown for every product: no vendor or user test of an infoframe-less (DVI-mode) input was found. The only evidence is for HDZero, whose HDMI receiver (ITE IT66021) is documented as backward compatible with DVI 1.0 and whose firmware measures the timing itself, using the AVI-style VIC only as an override (VIC 4/16 forces 60 Hz, VIC 19/31 forces 50 Hz), with colour space read from what appears to be the AVI InfoFrame (register mapping inferred) and a value-0 default path. A wrong VIC would therefore be worse than none.

### Cited Findings
- IT66021FN datasheet (Preliminary V0.91, Jun 2014; shipped in the HDZero repo): "fully compatible with HDMI 1.4b, HDMI 1.4b 3D and HDCP 1.4 and also backward compatible to DVI 1.0 specifications"; "DVI 1.0 supports only RGB color space" (hence the built-in bidirectional colour-space conversion); "Without software intervention the hardware ... should be able to output some sort of displayable video data in HDMI mode. However, this video could be in the wrong format or color space." Link clock up to 300 MHz, integrated programmable EDID RAM. [MFR chip datasheet] — [IT66021FN datasheet in repo](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/blob/adb901d9493a3f3d0ca3b052eaa693e51046dd3a/docs/IT66021FN_Preliminary_Datasheet_V0.91.pdf)
- HDZero timing detection reads the receiver's own counters (a pixel-clock reference register read five times and sorted, H total, V total, H active, V active) and computes fps = (6831 / ref) x 1e6 / htotal / vtotal. It then switches to bank 2 register 0x18 and, if the value is 16 or 4, forces fps = 60, or if 19 or 31, forces fps = 50. These numbers are CEA-861 VICs (1080p60, 720p60, 720p50, 1080p50) and the commit is titled "detect hdmi in fps by CEA-861-D" (2024-08-19; PR #435 merged 2024-09-03; shipped in v9.5.0). With no InfoFrame the override simply does not fire and the measured value is used. [CODE] — [it66021.c](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/blob/adb901d9493a3f3d0ca3b052eaa693e51046dd3a/src/driver/it66021.c#L221-L288), [commit e50c233](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/commit/e50c233bd4372059e9ca5c4590498caff6c5a7b3), [v9.5.0 release notes](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/releases/tag/v9.5.0)
- HDZero reads colour space from bank 2 register 0x15 bits [6:5] (the AVI "Y" field position), and on change programs the CSC matrix: value 0 writes 0x02 to register 0x65 (mask 0x03), any other value writes 0x00. [CODE] — [it66021.c, Get_CS/Set_CSMatrix](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/blob/adb901d9493a3f3d0ca3b052eaa693e51046dd3a/src/driver/it66021.c#L290-L308)
- HDZero re-initialises the display path only when the classified mode changes (vtmg_last compared to vtmg), not on each frame; classification depends only on active size and the fps bin, so a +/-1 line change in V total does not by itself trigger a re-init in firmware. [CODE] — [hardware-goggle.c, HDMI_in_detect](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/blob/adb901d9493a3f3d0ca3b052eaa693e51046dd3a/src/driver/hardware-goggle.c#L1001-L1150)
- EDID the HDZero goggle presents to sources (shipped up to v9.5.0, as removed in the Feb 2026 patch): manufacturer ID ITE, monitor name "ITE6802", range limits V 48 to 122 Hz, H 15 to 80 kHz, max pixel clock 160 MHz; preferred detailed timing 1920x1080 at 50.000 Hz (148.5 MHz, 2640x1125, odd front porch of 88); second timing 1280x720 at 60.000 Hz (74.25 MHz, 1650x750); CEA extension video data block VIC 4 (720p60), 31 (1080p50), 41 (720p100), 16 (1080p60), all flagged native; basic-audio flag and a 2-channel LPCM audio block. Decoded by the researcher from the byte arrays. [CODE] — [commit f1b6c45](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/commit/f1b6c45490e668cde448edb3f78175c03e7bb95a)
- Community EDID patch merged 2026-03-13 (PR #601, in pre-release v9.6.0-rc1) says it adds "1280x720 @ 50 Hz" and "960x720 @ 100 Hz (CVT-RB)" timings. Decoding the bytes shows the third timing is actually 1280x720 with 1650x750 totals at 74.65 MHz (60.32 Hz) and the fourth is 1920x1080 with 2200x1125 totals at 245.76 MHz (99.3 Hz), i.e. the comments and the bytes disagree. Only matters to sources that read EDID. [CODE; researcher decode] — [PR #601](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/pull/601), [v9.6.0-rc1](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/releases/tag/v9.6.0-rc1)
- HDZero v9.5.x user reports relevant to a 720p RGB source: "HDMI brightness incorrect at 720p in v9.5" (blown-out blacks at 720p60 and 720p100 with a Walksnail VRX and a PC; 1080p60 correct; downgrading to 9.4 fixed it; still open and still reproducible after a board swap on 2025-11-07; reporter suspects PR #430, "modify clock phase of hdmi in"). [USER, open] — [issue #502](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/issues/502)
- HDZero HDMI-in needs a cable replug to be detected ("common issue ... since day one"; workaround: power the external source after the goggles; last comment 2026-03-30); random black or garbled screens at 1080p60 with a Walksnail VRX and a RunCam WiFiLink2 VRX, with suggested causes (OLED sleep setting, Walksnail high-frame-rate mode as a workaround) and no fix recorded. [USER, open] — [issue #449](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/issues/449), [issue #445](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/issues/445)
- Caddx's VRX HDMI-output page says nothing about InfoFrames or audio. [MFR] — [Caddx VRX page](https://www.caddxfpv.com/blogs/news/walksnail-avatar-vrx-hdmi-digital-fpv-receiver)
- No manual or spec found for Skyzone, Fat Shark, Orqa, Walksnail Goggles X, Eachine or Goovis mentions DVI mode, InfoFrames or EDID behaviour (the Skyzone Cobra manual only says the detected resolution is shown on screen). [MFR] — [Skyzone Cobra manual](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0597/9668/4956/files/COBRA_260907.pdf?v=1789970381)

### Inferences
- On HDZero hardware a source with no AVI InfoFrame should be classified purely by measured size and rate (so 1280x720 at 59.94/60 Hz becomes "720P60", 50 Hz becomes "720P50"), with colour treated as the default (RGB) path. This follows from the code and the DVI-compatible chip, but has not been confirmed by a test.
- A source that does send an InfoFrame must send a VIC that matches its timing: VIC 19 on a 60 Hz signal would be forced to 50 Hz, VIC 4 on a 50 Hz signal forced to 60 Hz. The VIC-41 (720p100) case is not overridden.
- The 750/751-line alternation changes the measured fps between about 60.0 and 59.9 Hz at a fixed 74.25 MHz clock; both fall in HDZero's 57 to 80 Hz "720P60" bin. Whether the IT66021's own stable-video logic tolerates a V total that changes every frame is unknown without a bench test.
- Blown-out blacks at 720p in v9.5.0 (issue #502) is a plausible range/colour-matrix problem; test with a ramp pattern on HDZero v9.5.0 versus v9.6.0-rc1.

### Gaps
- No test of infoframe-less input on any goggle. Needed: a bench test with the signal and AVI InfoFrame on/off, VIC absent/correct/wrong, on at least HDZero (v9.5.0 and v9.6.0-rc1), Skyzone SKY04X Pro, Fat Shark HDO2/HDO+, Orqa FPV.One Pilot and Walksnail Goggles X.
- The IT66021 programming guide is not in the repo (only the datasheet and IT66121 documents), so the bank 2 register meanings (0x15, 0x18) are inferred from the VIC values and the commit title, not from register documentation.
- The IT66021 EDID RAM content in release firmware v9.5.0 vs v9.6.0-rc1 differs; the release EDID for the Goggle 2 and BoxPro builds was not separately checked.

## Key question 4: What HDMI receiver / scaler chips are used (teardowns, FCC internal photos, firmware strings)?

### Takeaway
Only the HDZero goggles are chip-identified from primary sources: ITE IT66021FN HDMI 1.4b receiver, ITE IT66121 HDMI transmitter, a "V536" SoC (Allwinner SDK), two FPGAs, TP2825-family analog decoders (plus a GM7150 in the I2C map) and HDZero's own DM5680/DM6302 ICs. For Skyzone, Fat Shark, Orqa, Walksnail, Eachine and Goovis no teardown, FCC internal photo or firmware-string analysis was found; the only hints are a Skyzone statement that an FPGA rotates the image in the Cobra X V2 and panel-vendor names for some Fat Shark models.

### Cited Findings
- HDZero HDMI receiver: ITE IT66021 at I2C 0x49 ("L_I2C ... IT66021 0x49 (HDMI IN)"), with its EDID RAM programmed over I2C 0x54; HDMI transmitter IT66121 at 0x4C. The IT66021FN is "Single-port HDMI1.4 Receiver with 3D Support", 76-pin QFN, 24-bit RGB/YCbCr 4:4:4, 16-bit 4:2:2 and BT.656 outputs. [CODE/MFR] — [misc/note](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/blob/adb901d9493a3f3d0ca3b052eaa693e51046dd3a/misc/note), [i2c.h](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/blob/adb901d9493a3f3d0ca3b052eaa693e51046dd3a/src/driver/i2c.h), [IT66021FN datasheet](https://github.com/hd-zero/hdzero-goggle/blob/adb901d9493a3f3d0ca3b052eaa693e51046dd3a/docs/IT66021FN_Preliminary_Datasheet_V0.91.pdf)
- HDZero manual: HDMI input is "a single port HDMI 1.4b receiver through a mini HDMI port ... routed to the OLED display without adding any frame buffer latency"; HDMI output is "a high-performance single channel HDMI transmitter that is fully compliant with HDMI 1.3a". [MFR] — [HDZero Goggle manual v2.1](https://www.hd-zero.com/_files/ugd/967e02_e569535d755544d4b0300dde2f13e21f.pdf)

### Inferences
- For the HDZero family the HDMI path is IT66021 receiver to an FPGA to the OLED ("routed to the OLED display without adding any frame buffer latency"), so any mapping of 720p onto the 1920x1080 panel is not done through a full frame buffer; the firmware selects separate screen.vtmg() and FPGA register settings per input class. The SoC's role (the firmware also sets the SoC video-output timing and the DVR input per class, probably for recording) and the exact scaling mechanism were not documented.
- Skyzone's HDMI path in Cobra-type goggles appears to include an FPGA that rotates the image (per Skyzone); whether the HDMI receiver on any Skyzone, Fat Shark, Orqa or Walksnail goggle is an ITE, Lontium, Toshiba or other part is unknown.

- No FCC internal photos, teardown or firmware-string analysis found for any non-HDZero product (search quota exhausted; FCC database lookups not done).

## Key question 5: What is the measured HDMI-input latency per goggle?

### Takeaway
Independent HDMI-input data is thin: one direct measurement (Skyzone Cobra X V2, 37 +/- 4 ms, Dec 2022, includes image rotation) and one set of system-level numbers through a Walksnail VRX (Feb 2023, +/-4 ms: SKY04X and HDO2 about 40 ms at 720p60, HDZero about 40 ms at 720p100, Orqa FPV.One about 52 ms, Cobra X about 76 ms, native digital baseline 32 ms). HDZero claims 1 ms for its HDMI input (manufacturer only). Nothing was measured for SKY04X Pro HDMI, HDO+, Goggles X HDMI-in, EV300 or Goovis.

### Cited Findings
- Cobra X V2 HDMI input: 37 +/- 4 ms, using a Raspberry Pi Zero input-lag tester filmed with a 240 fps camera (Pixel 5); the poster notes it "combines" HDMI input lag and image-processing lag and estimates HDMI alone at about 14 ms (37 minus a 23 ms analog figure), adding it "will vary quite a lot from goggle to goggle" (mihaiko, 3 and 20 Dec 2022, pre-2024). Analog on the same unit by Chris Rosser's method: 23 +/- 6 ms (first bright pixel) and 35 +/- 6 ms (first bright field); a Fat Shark Scout measured 40 +/- 5 and 55 +/- 6 ms. [MEAS] — [IntoFPV thread](https://intofpv.com/archive/index.php/thread-22188.html)
- Cobra X V2 with a Walksnail VRX in 720p60 (firmware 3.4.9): 71 ms (first bright pixel) and 83 ms (full frame), SD 6 ms; another user reported "about 50..60ms" HDMI lag on a Cobra X V2 as an estimate with no method (13 Feb 2023). [MEAS/USER] — [IntoFPV thread](https://intofpv.com/archive/index.php/thread-22188.html)
- Oscar Liang, Walksnail VRX review (Feb 2023, pre-2024): GoPro Hero 11 at 240 fps counting frames until an LED lights, "up to 4ms of error". Avatar Goggles (or Fat Shark Dominator HD) at 720p100 and 25 Mbps about 32 ms, "better than any goggles connected to the VRX via HDMI"; Skyzone SKY04X and Fat Shark HDO2 at 720p60 and HDZero goggles at 720p100 "around 40ms"; Orqa FPV.ONE "around 52ms"; Skyzone Cobra X "76ms". [REVIEW/USER/MFR] — [Oscar Liang SKY04X Pro](https://oscarliang.com/skyzone-sky04x-pro/), [Skyzone downloads](https://www.skyzonefpv.com/pages/download)

[USER] — [IntoFPV thread](https://intofpv.com/t-ws-goggles-vs-hdzero-goggles-w-ws-vrx), [Oscar Liang EV300D](https://oscarliang.com/eachine-ev300d-fpv-goggles/)

### Inferences
- The only isolated HDMI-input number is the Cobra X V2 figure, and it includes a Skyzone-acknowledged 90-degree FPGA rotation, so it should not be generalised to other goggles.
- At the same 100 fps mode, HDZero's 40 ms versus the 32 ms native baseline leaves about 8 ms for the VRX's HDMI output plus the goggle input; that is consistent with a very small HDMI-in delay but is within the +/-4 ms measurement error of a "1 ms" claim, so it neither confirms nor refutes it.
- For a 60 Hz source one frame is 16.7 ms: any goggle that buffers a frame to rescale 720p to its panel would add at least that much, which may explain why the Orqa (about 52 ms) and Cobra X (about 76 ms) results are higher than the HDZero/Skyzone/Fat Shark OLED results; this is a hypothesis, not a measurement.

### Gaps
- No HDMI-input latency was found for SKY04X Pro, HDO+, Goggles X (HDMI-in), Orqa alone, EV300O/D, Goovis, or for any DVI/59.94 Hz source; Joshua Bardwell and Chris Rosser latency videos are not available as text, so their numbers were not retrieved.
- All 2022-2023 numbers are pre-2024 and predate current firmware (for example Skyzone V4.2.x, HDZero v9.5.0).

- Suggested bench-test priority for a 720p DVI-style source: (1) HDZero Goggle or Goggle 2 (best documented, chip known, firmware inspectable), (2) Skyzone SKY04X Pro (largest installed base, 720p100/1080p60 spec), (3) Orqa FPV.One Pilot (strictest, 720p60 only), (4) Walksnail Goggles X (1080p-oriented, PC-monitor behaviour), (5) legacy HDO2 and Cobra X V4 for older Skyzone/Fat Shark hardware, (6) Goovis G3 Max as a non-FPV HMD with an adapter in the path.
- Panels span 640x400 (SKY02O) to 2560x1440 per eye (G3 Max); for 1280x720 input only the SKY04O Pro, Cobra X (both 1280x720 native) and the Orqa (16:9 720p mode) avoid scaling, while 1920x1080 OLED goggles must up-scale 720p, which is where a frame buffer (extra latency) may or may not be used.
