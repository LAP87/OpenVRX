# Bridges, signal rules and sink behaviour for feeding XR glasses and goggles from an FPGA HDMI/DVI source

*Research date: 2026-10-12. Scope: Tang Nano 20K HDMI connector, DVI-style TMDS (video only, no InfoFrames), 1280x720 at 60.00 Hz (1650x750), a genlocked 59.94 Hz mode with 750/751-line frames, and 720p50 (1980x750).*
*Evidence tags: **[SPEC]** standard text as quoted by the cited page; **[VENDOR]** vendor datasheet/brief/support page; **[CODE]** open-source code or header read directly; **[USER]** forum, Reddit or blog report; **[MEAS]** measurement (none found for the bridges); **(2nd-hand)** the page itself could not be opened by me (blocked, redirected or unreachable) and the fact comes from a web-search result summary, so treat it as unverified until re-checked. "Link omitted" means a retailer page, withheld because the brief forbids shop links. Dates are given where known so older material can be discounted.*
*Research limits: the web-search quota ran out near the end of the session, several primary pages were blocked (Anubis bot wall, HTTP 403/401/404), and Reddit mirrors were unreachable. Gaps are listed per question.*

## 1. HDMI-to-USB-C DisplayPort bridges and adapters for XR glasses (chips, scaling, latency, power, compatibility, price)

### Takeaway
The only HDMI-in to USB-C DP Alt Mode bridge silicon I could verify is Lontium's LT6711 family (found inside a teardown adapter); its public product brief describes a plain HDMI 2.0 receiver to DP 1.2 transmitter with Type-C/PD and says nothing about scaling, latency, EDID handling or DVI/InfoFrames. The other parts named in the brief could not be verified as HDMI-in bridges (ANX7625 is MIPI-in, the ITE and Realtek parts found convert DP/USB-C to HDMI, IT6225/LT6711C/AG9321 were found nowhere), XREAL Beam/Beam Pro/Hub and Rokid Station/Hub have no HDMI input, and I found no measured latency, no 720p/50 Hz statement and no first-party price for any HDMI-to-USB-C adapter.

### Inferences
- The FPGA path is HDMI(DVI) -> bridge (LT6711-class, or VITURE's own adapter for VITURE glasses) -> DP Alt Mode -> glasses' DP receiver/scaler. XREAL Beam, Beam Pro, Hub and Rokid Station/Hub are not in this path because they have no HDMI input.
- The LT6711 brief mentions no scaler or frame buffer, so a line/pixel-FIFO-level delay (well under a frame) is plausible, but this is absence of evidence in a "product brief", not a measurement. Treat adapter latency as unknown until measured with the project's latency rig.
- 74.25 MHz (742.5 Mb/s per TMDS lane) is far below the LT6711 receiver's 6 Gb/s lane ceiling, but the brief does not list a minimum pixel clock or any 720p-specific support; 720p is a CTA-861 mainstream format, so rejection would be unusual but cannot be excluded.
- The glasses' own EDID (if the XREAL Air sample is representative) lists no 720p; whether a bridge copies that EDID to its HDMI side or presents a fixed one is undocumented. The Tang Nano source ignores EDID anyway, so the real question is whether the glasses' scaler accepts a DP stream whose timing is not in its EDID.
- Part numbers LT6711C, IT6225 and AG9321 may be mistyped, unreleased or OEM-only; do not design around them.

## 2. How HDMI receivers, DP bridges and scalers react to a 59.94 Hz stream with alternating 750/751-line frames; does DisplayPort MSA tolerate a changing Vtotal?

### Takeaway
I found no vendor, specification or user document describing how any HDMI receiver, HDMI-to-DP bridge (LT6711 etc.) or XR glasses react to a per-frame Vtotal change in a non-VRR stream, so this is an untested risk rather than a known fact. What is documented: DisplayPort carries Htotal/Vtotal in MSA and its formal route to per-frame Vtotal changes is Adaptive-Sync with the sink's "ignore MSA timing parameters" capability (DPCD 0x007 bit 6, enabled by DPCD 0x107 bit 7), and real sinks are reported to take anywhere from 1 s to 30 s to recover from timing or pixel-clock changes.

### Cited Findings
#### DisplayPort MSA and the Ignore-MSA capability
- [CODE] Linux `drm_dp.h`: DPCD 0x007 `DP_DOWN_STREAM_PORT_COUNT` bit 6 = `DP_MSA_TIMING_PAR_IGNORED` (annotated eDP); DPCD 0x107 `DP_DOWNSPREAD_CTRL` bit 7 = `DP_MSA_TIMING_PAR_IGNORE_EN` (annotated eDP); MSA MISC bit definitions (`DP_MSA_MISC_SYNC_CLOCK` bit 0, `DP_MSA_MISC_INTERLACE_VTOTAL_EVEN` bit 8, bit-depth/colour fields); Adaptive-Sync SDP type 0x22 "DP 1.4" with AS-SDP operating modes "AVT dynamic vtotal" / "AVT fixed vtotal" / FAVT. — [drm_dp.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/master/include/drm/display/drm_dp.h)
- (2nd-hand) Linux patch "drm/dp: DRM DP helper for reading Ignore MSA from DPCD": a sink sets the bit to show it can disregard the MSA video timing parameters and accept seamless timing changes over a range listed in its EDID/DisplayID; the bit is required for the sink to indicate Adaptive-Sync capability. — [openSUSE kernel-source mirror](https://code.opensuse.org/kernel/kernel-source/blob/0b62bdb4751d0cd6898a3176acfedaa5d21e9d3a/f/patches.suse/0234-drm-dp-DRM-DP-helper-for-reading-Ignore-MSA-from-DPC.patch)
- (2nd-hand; DP 1.2a era, older) AMD's DisplayPort 1.2a Specification Change Request, reposted by Blur Busters: extends the "MSA timing parameter ignore" option from eDP to external DP so that the sink slaves its timing to the source for per-frame dynamic refresh; Table 3-7 lists the MSA parameters that may be ignored, including HTotal, VTotal, sync start/width and polarities; single-stream devices only; sink advertises the capability in EDID; the source must parse EDID before enabling it. — [Blur Busters SCR article](https://www.blurbusters.com/displayport-spec-change-request-for-variable-refresh/), [forum repost](https://forums.blurbusters.com/viewtopic.php?p=2162)
- (2nd-hand) Intel DisplayPort IP User Guide (sink/RX side): the MSA interface exposes h_total, v_total, start/sync fields and polarities; `msa_valid` deasserts during an MSA update; `msa_lock` asserts once the MSA fields have been correctly formatted for the last 15 video frames; the receiver can strobe new MSA values after an idle video period, for example when the source changes resolution. — [Intel DP IP guide, MSA interface](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/programmable/683273/25-3/msa-interface.html); MSA field list also in the Xilinx LogiCORE DP TX driver: [U-Boot logicore_dp_tx.h](https://elixir.u-boot.org/u-boot/master/source/drivers/video/logicore_dp_tx.h)
- [VENDOR] The LT6711 brief says nothing about MSA handling, Adaptive-Sync/VRR or Ignore-MSA.

#### HDMI timing facts and tolerances
- [CODE] CEA-861 timings from the Linux V4L2 header (read directly, master branch on 2026-10-12): 1280x720p60 = pixel clock 74,250,000 Hz, H front porch/sync/back porch 110/40/220 (Htotal 1650), V 5/5/20 (Vtotal 750), +HSYNC/+VSYNC, VIC 4, flags CAN_REDUCE_FPS | IS_CE_VIDEO; 1280x720p50 = same clock, H front porch 440 (Htotal 1980), V 5/5/20, VIC 19, no CAN_REDUCE_FPS flag; 1920x1080p60 = 148.5 MHz, Htotal 2200 / Vtotal 1125, VIC 16 (CAN_REDUCE_FPS); 1920x1080p50 = 148.5 MHz, Htotal 2640 / Vtotal 1125, VIC 31. — [v4l2-dv-timings.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/master/include/uapi/linux/v4l2-dv-timings.h)
- [CODE/README] The hdl-util FPGA HDMI core lists VIC 4 at 74.25 MHz (60 Hz) and 74.176 MHz (59.94 Hz), VIC 19 at 74.25 MHz (50 Hz): i.e. 59.94 Hz is conventionally produced by dividing the pixel clock by 1.001, not by changing line count. — [hdl-util/hdmi README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hdl-util/hdmi/master/README.md)
- [SPEC] (2nd-hand; HDMI 1.2 text quoted by a search tool, older spec) "An HDMI Sink that accepts 60Hz video formats, and that supports HDTV capability, shall support 1280x720p @ 59.94/60Hz or 1920x1080i @ 59.94/60Hz video format timings": 59.94 and 60 Hz are one format in the sink requirement. — [HDMI 1.2 section 6](https://source.netsyms.com/skylarmt/glenwing.github.io_withhdmi/raw/branch/master/docs/HDMI-1.2-sec6.pdf), [HDMI 1.4 section 6](https://source.netsyms.com/skylarmt/glenwing.github.io_withhdmi/raw/branch/master/docs/HDMI-1.4-sec6.pdf)
- [USER/developer] (Mar 2017) Linux V4L2 maintainer Hans Verkuil: the pixel-clock difference between 60 and 59.94 Hz "is about 0.1%"; "the HDMI spec has an error of margin for the pixelclock of (I think) 0.25%"; receivers find the two rates hard to tell apart ("some of the older Analog Devices devices didn't have the resolution to detect the difference"). He is unsure of the 0.25 % figure. — [LKML, v4l2_calc_fps thread](https://lkml.iu.edu/1703.1/06535.html)
- (2nd-hand) A per-frame Vtotal change is exactly what VRR does: "nearly all VRR technologies simply use a dynamically-variable Vertical Back Porch"; drivers often refuse to output VRR to a display that does not advertise it, though forced output via an EDID override works on some GPUs. An Intel HDMI sink-IP guide notes that because VRR varies vertical blanking at run time, the sink keeps `vid_lock` asserted for VRR transport; an AMD Linux patch series (2026) reports blanking when a sink's advertised VRR range is wrong. — [Blur Busters, "Genericness of VRR"](https://forums.blurbusters.com/viewtopic.php?p=69658), [Intel HDMI IP guide, VRR](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/programmable/683798/22-1-19-7-0/variable-refresh-rate-vrr-and-auto-low-81928.html), [LKML AMD VRR/HDMI gaming-features series](https://lkml.iu.edu/2602.2/00864.html)

#### Observed sink reactions to unusual timing
- [CODE/docs] MiSTer.ini (FPGA project with a genlock-style HDMI output): `vsync_adjust=1` = "automatic HDMI VSync rate adjust to match original VSync", `=2` = "low latency mode (single buffer)"; "Adjusting is done by changing pixel clock. Not every display supports variable pixel clock."; "For proper adjusting and to reduce possible out of range pixel clock, use 60Hz HDMI video modes as a base even for 50Hz systems."; `refresh_min`/`refresh_max` exist so adjustment is not applied outside a monitor's range ("NTSC monitors may not support PAL"). — [MiSTer.ini](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MiSTer-devel/Main_MiSTer/master/MiSTer.ini)
- [USER] (Mar 2024) MiSTer forum, Dell G2723H monitor: with `vsync_adjust=2` the monitor lost sync for "about 10-15 seconds" on core/game loads while an LG 4K TV lost it for 1-2 s; `vrr_mode=1` made it 20-30 s; `vrr_mode=2` with the monitor's range (48-240) fixed loss on game loads and in-game resolution changes, but core loading still took about 32 s with every cable and setting; the user concluded the monitor was at fault and returned it. — [MiSTer forum thread](https://mail.misterfpga.org/viewtopic.php?p=84360)
- [USER, indirect] (Nov 2022-Jan 2023) OSSC forum: a board with an unstable HSync (13.572 <-> 13.722 kHz, about 275 lines/frame) lost sync within a couple of seconds; OSSC developer: the unstable period is "challenging to any video digitizer" and there is "no guarantee" a regenerated pixel clock is accepted by the display. This concerns the scaler's input side, so it is weak evidence for sink behaviour.
- (2nd-hand) RetroTINK-4K sync modes as relayed by ProVideo Coalition: Frame Lock ties output frame rate tightly to input (no dropped/duplicated frames, but input disruptions pass straight to the output), Gen Lock couples more loosely, TBC loosest, Triple Buffer free-runs at 60 Hz and drops/duplicates frames. How the output timing is varied is not stated. — [ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/retrotink-5x-pro-tbc-scaler-with-blackmagic-video-assist-to-capture-upscale-legacy-analog-to-1080p-in-realtime/)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic for the planned streams (computed from the 1650 x 750 / 74.25 MHz CEA timing above; one line = 22.222 us): 750 lines = 16.667 ms = 60.000 Hz; 751 lines = 16.689 ms = 59.920 Hz; to average 59.94006 Hz at a fixed 74.25 MHz the mean must be 750.75 lines, i.e. about 3 of 4 frames carry 751 lines; a strict 750/751/750/751 alternation averages 750.5 lines = 59.960 Hz; a constant 750 lines at 59.94006 Hz needs a 74.1758 MHz pixel clock (74.25 / 1.001). One extra line is 0.133 % of a frame, similar in size to the 0.1 % 59.94/60 offset, and the TMDS clock itself stays at exactly 74.25 MHz; only VSYNC spacing changes.
- Because Vtotal is carried in every MSA, a bridge that rebuilds the DP stream from the measured HDMI input will either (a) pass a changing Vtotal downstream every frame, (b) latch Vtotal once and keep it (then the DP stream drifts against its own timing and a FIFO must slip), or (c) detect a timing change and re-initialise the link (blank or flicker). The LT6711 brief does not say which. A DP sink that treats MSA changes as a new video mode (as RX IP such as Intel's `msa_lock` logic does) could drop lock; only sinks advertising Adaptive-Sync/Ignore-MSA are specified to accept it.
- HDMI-side equivalent: a sink that classifies the input by exact Vtotal/Hz against a VIC table may toggle between "720p60" and "unknown" with 750/751 alternation; scaler-type sinks that re-measure each frame and drive a fixed-rate panel from a frame buffer should simply drop or repeat one frame about every 1000 frames. Neither behaviour is documented for XR glasses.
- The MiSTer evidence says sinks vary hugely (1 s vs 30+ s) in how they recover from pixel-clock changes, and that the project itself ships the most compatible mode as the default (buffered 60 Hz); this supports testing "constant 750 lines, fixed pixel clock" modes first and treating line-count genlock as the last, riskiest option.
- Lower-risk alternatives to per-frame 750/751 (all unverified in this research): (i) pixel clock 74.1758 MHz with constant 750 lines and slip one frame occasionally; (ii) add the extra line only rarely (for example one frame in N) so the sink sees a timing change far less often; (iii) trim the pixel clock slowly (MiSTer approach) if the FPGA PLL allows it. Each trades latency jitter against sink risk.

### Gaps
- No documentation of any HDMI receiver, bridge (LT6711 etc.) or XR glasses behaviour under per-frame Vtotal changes, and no vendor tolerance figure ("lines of Vtotal variation accepted").
- The normative DisplayPort text (MSA transmission and update rules, Table 3-7, Ignore-MSA behaviour) is behind VESA membership; only second-hand summaries and kernel headers were reachable.
- Whether LT6711-class bridges re-measure input timing every frame, latch it, or re-train on change: unknown.
- Whether the glasses' DP receivers treat an MSA change as a mode change: unknown.
- The 0.25 % HDMI pixel-clock tolerance is Verkuil's unconfirmed recollection; the HDMI spec's own tolerance text was not read.

## 3. DVI-style TMDS without AVI InfoFrames: acceptance by HDMI-input goggles and HDMI-to-DP adapters; what the HDMI spec requires of a sink

### Takeaway
HDMI sinks are specified to accept native single-link DVI video, and CTA-861 defines default behaviour when no AVI InfoFrame is present, so a DVI-style 720p stream is legal input; no source I found documents any XR glasses, goggles or HDMI-to-DP bridge that requires InfoFrames (nor confirms that one accepts their absence). The practically important side effect is range/colour ambiguity: 720p is a CE timing, and without an AVI InfoFrame a sink must guess limited vs full range.

### Cited Findings
- [SPEC, tertiary] "HDMI is backward compatible with single-link Digital Visual Interface digital video" (DVI-D/DVI-I; not DVI-A or dual-link); "HDMI is electrically compatible with DVI video signals"; under HDMI 1.0: "All HDMI sources/sinks must also be capable of sending/receiving native Single-Link DVI video"; HDMI interleaves video data, data-island and control periods (audio and auxiliary data travel in data islands); HPD is pin 19, DDC (E-DDC) is required, CEC wiring is mandatory; HDCP can block DVI interoperability for protected content; HDMI 1.0's maximum TMDS clock is 165 MHz, single-link rates start at 25 MHz. — [Wikipedia: HDMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDMI)
- [SPEC] (2nd-hand; HDMI 1.2/1.4 section 6, older specs, wording as relayed by a search tool) sink video-format requirements are keyed to frame-rate family: a sink accepting 60 Hz-family formats must support 640x480p and 720x480p at 59.94/60 Hz and, if HDTV-capable, 720p or 1080i at 59.94/60 Hz; a sink accepting 50 Hz-family formats must support 576p50 and, if HDTV-capable, 720p50 or 1080i50. Source-side rules are broader (at least one of the basic SD formats plus the 720p and 1080i formats for its family, and any format the source outputs on other uncompressed outputs). — [HDMI 1.2 section 6](https://source.netsyms.com/skylarmt/glenwing.github.io_withhdmi/raw/branch/master/docs/HDMI-1.2-sec6.pdf), [HDMI 1.4 section 6](https://source.netsyms.com/skylarmt/glenwing.github.io_withhdmi/raw/branch/master/docs/HDMI-1.4-sec6.pdf)
- [USER/developer] (Jan 2018) Hans Verkuil on a receiver driver: "The CTA-861 spec says that in the absence of an AVI InfoFrame you should use limited range for CE formats and full range for IT formats", "without an AVI InfoFrame it is hard to detect a CE format since there is no VIC code", "a real DVI source will always transmit full range", and he recommends falling back to sRGB full range, "Not quite according to the spec but more likely to work in practice." — [LKML, TDA1997x review](https://lkml.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/1801.3/01285.html)
- (2nd-hand) Kernel V4L2 docs keep manual limited/full overrides because sinks that have not implemented the range rules correctly are "unfortunately quite common for HDMI and DVI-D"; a Rockchip HDMI patch notes some HDMI modes must be sent as limited-range RGB and the range signalled in the AVI InfoFrame for sinks that do not support non-standard ranges. — [V4L2 DV controls](https://kernel.org/doc/html/v6.2/userspace-api/media/v4l/ext-ctrls-dv.html), [LKML inno_hdmi patch](https://lkml.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/2312.2/07887.html)
- [CODE/README] hdl-util/hdmi: "Many free and open source ... implementations output DVI, which HDMI sinks accept" (backwards compatibility); audio and other HDMI features need "a true HDMI signal"; a `DVI_OUTPUT` parameter disables HDMI-only logic; AVI, Audio and SPD InfoFrames are implemented; the core is hotplug-unaware, EDID is not implemented ("format is fixed at synthesis time"); the only sink named is a Dell UltraSharp monitor; the README does not claim monitors or TVs require InfoFrames. — [hdl-util/hdmi README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hdl-util/hdmi/master/README.md)
- [CODE/docs] MiSTer.ini offers `dvi_mode`: "set to 1 for DVI mode. Audio won't be transmitted through HDMI in DVI mode." — [MiSTer.ini](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MiSTer-devel/Main_MiSTer/master/MiSTer.ini)
- [CODE/README] The NESTang README for Tang boards (including Nano 20K) advertises "720p HDMI output with sound", so a Tang Nano 20K has been used with HDMI-mode output (data islands) at 720p; it gives no sink-compatibility notes. — [nestang README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nand2mario/nestang/master/README.md)
- [USER] (2nd-hand) A Tang Nano 9K builder on the NABU forum found a bitstream had poor compatibility with TVs that are "picky" about standards while PC HDMI monitors accepted it; a mikrocontroller.net thread ("Display Controller funktioniert nur mit DVI und nicht HDMI") documents the reverse mismatch (a DVI-only sink rejecting HDMI-mode signalling). — [NABU forum](https://forums.nabu.ca/viewtopic.php?p=1090), [mikrocontroller.net](https://www.mikrocontroller.net/topic/362372)
- [VENDOR] The LT6711 brief calls its input an "HDMI2.0 Receiver" with SCDC/HDCP and says nothing about DVI mode or InfoFrame requirements; the XREAL Connect Devices page and the VITURE adapter page also say nothing about DVI or InfoFrames. — [XREAL tutorials](https://tutorials.xreal.com/docs/glasses/one-series/first-use/connect-device), [VITURE Academy](https://www.viture.com/academy/adapters/hdmi-xr-adapter)
- [VENDOR] The Sipeed Tang Nano 20K wiki page shows an "HDMI interface" but its text does not say whether DDC, HPD or +5 V are wired (pin diagram and schematic are images/links). — [Sipeed wiki, Tang Nano 20K](https://wiki.sipeed.com/hardware/en/tang/tang-nano-20k/nano-20k.html)

### Inferences
- Spec-wise, an HDMI-input goggle or an HDMI 2.0 receiver such as the LT6711's should accept DVI-style 720p60 TMDS; the failure modes to expect are not "no signal" from missing InfoFrames but (a) wrong range/contrast (a spec-strict sink may assume limited range for the CE timing 720p, a DVI-sniffing sink full range), and (b) rare sinks that only lock when they see HDMI signalling. If goggles show crushed or washed-out contrast, add an AVI InfoFrame (VIC 4 or 19, RGB, explicit range) and compare.
- An AVI InfoFrame cannot express the alternating 750/751 pattern; the VIC stays 4. InfoFrames would give a bridge chip a clean colour-format/range description for the DP MISC bits, which is a plausible reason adapters might behave better with them, but no source says so.
- For bridges, check hardware basics before blaming signalling: whether the board's connector provides +5 V on pin 18 and a readable DDC/HPD path (unverified for the Tang Nano 20K; general HDMI practice, not sourced here), because source-detect and EDID reads are how adapters wake up and choose a mode. The hdl-util core being "hotplug unaware" is a reminder that FPGA sources usually ignore HPD/EDID entirely.
- Testing order: (1) DVI-style stream, (2) same stream plus AVI InfoFrame (use the hdl-util core or NESTang's HDMI TX as a known-good reference), (3) compare picture range and lock time on each sink.

### Gaps
- No source states whether any XR glasses, HDMI-input FPV goggles or HDMI-to-DP bridge requires an AVI InfoFrame or VIC; none documents the range assumed when it is absent.
- The primary HDMI/CTA-861 text on sink DVI obligations and AVI defaults was not read (only Wikipedia, kernel discussion and search summaries).
- HDCP behaviour of bridges toward an unencrypted DVI source is undocumented (no source says they pass or reject it).
- DDC / HPD / +5 V wiring of the Tang Nano 20K HDMI connector is unverified (needs the schematic PDF).

## 4. 50 Hz acceptance by USB-C DP glasses and by HDMI goggles made for 60 Hz panels

### Takeaway
I found no document or report showing that any XR glasses, HDMI adapter or HDMI-input goggle accepts (or rejects) 720p50; the HDMI specification only obliges a sink to accept the 60 Hz family or the 50 Hz family it chooses to support, and the one glasses EDID summary found lists only 1080p 60/90/120 Hz. 50 Hz is therefore an untested path that must be checked per device, ideally before debugging the FPGA.

### Cited Findings
- [SPEC] (2nd-hand; HDMI 1.2/1.4 section 6, older specs) the sink rules are per frame-rate family: a sink that accepts 60 Hz-family formats must support the 60 Hz basic formats, one that accepts 50 Hz-family formats must support the 50 Hz ones; the HD requirement is either 720p or 1080i within the family, so a 50 Hz-capable sink can comply with 1080i50 and no 720p50. Region is not named in the requirement. — [HDMI 1.4 section 6](https://source.netsyms.com/skylarmt/glenwing.github.io_withhdmi/raw/branch/master/docs/HDMI-1.4-sec6.pdf), [HDMI 1.2 section 6](https://source.netsyms.com/skylarmt/glenwing.github.io_withhdmi/raw/branch/master/docs/HDMI-1.2-sec6.pdf)
- [CODE] VIC 19 = 1280x720p50, pixel clock 74.25 MHz, Htotal 1980 / Vtotal 750 (listed as a normal CEA-861 format, without the 1/1.001 flag); VIC 31 = 1920x1080p50 at 148.5 MHz. — [v4l2-dv-timings.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/master/include/uapi/linux/v4l2-dv-timings.h)
- [CODE/docs] MiSTer.ini treats 50 Hz as the risky case: it recommends a 60 Hz HDMI base mode "even for 50Hz systems" and says "NTSC monitors may not support PAL". — [MiSTer.ini](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MiSTer-devel/Main_MiSTer/master/MiSTer.ini)
- (2nd-hand) XREAL Air sample EDID: detailed timings 1920x1080 at 60, 90 and 120 Hz only, no CTA extension, no standard timings (so no 720p and no 50 Hz entry). — [freedesktop.org issue 53](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/drm/misc/kernel/-/issues/53)
- (2nd-hand, retailer sheets, links omitted) XREAL Beam Pro drives the glasses at 3840x1080@72 Hz and 1920x1080@90 Hz, i.e. the glasses are used at refresh rates other than 60 Hz, but nothing below 60 Hz is listed.
- (2nd-hand; professional equipment) A Barco projector manual lists 720p at 50, 59.94 and 60 Hz as supported HDMI timings. — [Barco manual, video timings](https://www.barco.com/manuals/R5916676/m7fakzfmd7my.html)
- (2nd-hand; 2013, old) The Leo Bodnar lag tester outputs only 1080p/60, so a standard lag tester cannot exercise a 50 Hz path.
- Searches for XREAL / VITURE / Rokid with "50Hz", "PAL", "720p50" or "576p" returned no user report either way. — (no source; negative result from this session's searches)

### Inferences
- A DP Alt Mode glasses' scaler may accept any CTA-861 timing it can resample to its panel rate, or it may only lock to modes in its EDID; the EDID summary suggests the second is plausible for the 1080p-only XREAL sample, but nothing proves either. HDMI-input goggles with 60 Hz panels may do frame-rate conversion internally (extra latency) or refuse.
- Ways to keep an HDMI sink that only likes 60 Hz usable with a 50 Hz camera (untested): present a 60 Hz-based mode and slip/duplicate frames, or present a non-standard long-blanking 50 Hz timing; each has its own risk and the first adds up to a frame of latency jitter.
- Quick discriminator: test 720p50 (VIC 19, 1980x750) from a PC first (see question 5). If the sink refuses it from a PC, the FPGA is not at fault.

### Gaps
- No evidence for any XR glasses or HDMI-input goggles (no FPV-goggle HDMI input surveyed in this session).
- XREAL / VITURE / Rokid support pages list no refresh-rate coverage for HDMI-adapter use (XREAL's Connect Devices page lists none).
- The HDMI/CTA-861 primary text on 50 Hz support was not read; region-versus-family wording is second-hand.

## 5. Practical testing method (EDID reading, forcing modes, glass-to-glass latency, cheap generators)

### Takeaway
Test in layers: read the sink's (or bridge's) EDID, drive the same sink from a PC with custom timings to separate "sink rejects the mode" from "FPGA signal is wrong", then run the FPGA through a small timing matrix, and finally measure latency with the project's high-speed-camera script. I found tools for the first two steps but no verified cheap hardware HDMI generator or tester with arbitrary timing control.

### Cited Findings
- [TOOL] edid-decode (Debian man page): reads stdin or hex dumps (from edid-decode, xrandr or Xorg logs); on modern Linux EDIDs are in `/sys/class/drm/*/edid`; `-c/--check` and `-C/--check-inline` report warnings/failures; `-S/--short-timings`, `-L/--long-timings`, `-n/--native-resolution`, `-p/--preferred-timings`; `-X/--xmodeline`, `-F/--fbmode`, `-V/--v4l2-timings` produce mode lines; `--vic <n>` and `--list-vics` show CTA-861 VIC timings; `-s/--skip-hex-dump`, `-H/--only-hex-dump`, `-o/--output-format` (hex, raw, carray, xml); `--cvt` and `--gtf` calculate reference timings. The page warns not all fields are decoded and validation opinions may be wrong. — [edid-decode(1)](https://manpages.debian.org/testing/edid-decode/edid-decode.1.en.html)
- [TOOL] Custom Resolution Utility (CRU, v1.5.3; main thread last edited 26 Jan 2026): "creates software EDID overrides in the registry and does not modify the hardware"; shows how a connected monitor defines its resolutions; adds detailed resolutions with timing modes (Manual, Automatic PC/HDTV/CRT, Native, Exact, Exact reduced, CVT/CVT-RB/GTF) and CTA-861 extension blocks; exports .bin/.dat/.inf/.txt/.csv/.exe; warns about pixel-clock limits of DVI/HDMI/DP adapters, low vertical blanking problems, and Windows 11 ignoring overrides for non-PnP displays with corrupted EDIDs. — [CRU thread, monitortests.com](https://www.monitortests.com/forum/Thread-Custom-Resolution-Utility-CRU)
- [TOOL, old] Monitor Asset Manager (EnTech, v2.9 of 28 Jun 2017, Windows): reads information directly from connected monitors, shows native/recommended timings, decodes EIA/CEA-861 extension data and DisplayID, can build EDID override INF files; the page does not say it dumps raw EDID. — [EnTech Monitor Asset Manager](https://www.entechtaiwan.com/util/moninfo.shtm)
- AW EDID Editor was named in the brief but its page could not be verified in this session.
- [PROJECT] `OpenVRX-Core/tests/latency/analyze_latency.py` (copies exist in OpenVRX-Core-ioreg and OpenVRX-Puck/core): measures glass-to-glass latency from a high-speed recording; film a blinking LED and the display(s) showing the FPV camera's view of it in one shot; the script tracks mean brightness in an LED region and one or more screen regions, finds rising edges (sub-frame interpolation), pairs each LED edge with the next screen edge and reports the delay; options include `--capture-fps`, `--led x,y,w,h`, repeated `--screen name=x,y,w,h`, and `--preview N` to write a frame with a coordinate grid. — local file `C:\Users\lenny\Desktop\Drönare\OpenVRX\OpenVRX-Core\tests\latency\analyze_latency.py`
- (2nd-hand) Reference lag testers: Leo Bodnar testers output 1080p at 60 Hz and the lag databases are 60 Hz-only; RTINGS uses a photodiode rig fed with a flashing square over HDMI/DP; TechRadar uses the Bodnar 4K tester. — [RTINGS](https://www.rtings.com/company/input-lag-tool), [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/televisions/how-we-test-tv-input-lag-at-techradar)
- [CODE/README] FPGA cores like hdl-util/hdmi do not implement EDID reading or hotplug, so an FPGA source cannot read the sink's EDID unless an I2C master on the DDC pins is added (and the connector wires them). — [hdl-util/hdmi README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hdl-util/hdmi/master/README.md)
- The Raspberry Pi `config.txt` documentation page was fetched but the `hdmi_timings` / `hdmi_mode=87` section was not in the readable part, and whether those options still work with the KMS driver could not be confirmed. — [Raspberry Pi config.txt docs](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/computers/config_txt.html)

### Inferences
- **Isolation plan.** (1) PC with DP Alt Mode USB-C -> glasses directly: dump EDID (Linux: `edid-decode -c` on `/sys/class/drm/card*-DP-*/edid`; Windows: Monitor Asset Manager or CRU's view) to see which modes the glasses advertise. (2) PC HDMI -> adapter -> glasses: dump again to see whether the adapter mirrors the glasses' EDID or presents its own. (3) With CRU (Windows) or `xrandr --newmode` / KMS mode lines (Linux) add: 1280x720 at 60.000 Hz (74.25 MHz, 1650x750, VIC 4), 59.94 Hz (74.1758 MHz), 50 Hz (1980x750, VIC 19), a constant 751-line variant (59.92 Hz); record accepted / blank / scaled / "unsupported". This separates sink and bridge limits from FPGA output faults.
- **FPGA timing matrix** (observe picture, blank flashes, re-sync time after hot-plug, any OSD text, 30-minute stability): T1 constant 1650x750 at 74.25 MHz (60.000 Hz); T2 constant 1650x750 at 74.1758 MHz (59.940 Hz); T3 constant 1650x751 at 74.25 MHz (59.92 Hz); T4 strict 750/751 alternation (59.96 Hz average); T5 three 751-line frames then one 750-line frame (59.94 Hz average); T6 a single 751-line frame every N frames (N = 10, 100, 1000) to see how a sink reacts to isolated Vtotal glitches; T7 the same set with an AVI InfoFrame; T8 1980x750 at 74.25 MHz (50.000 Hz). A PC cannot easily emit per-frame Vtotal changes to a non-VRR sink, so T4-T6 need the FPGA.
- **Latency.** Use the project's script with one `--led` region and separate `--screen` regions for each stage (reference monitor on the FPGA's HDMI via a splitter vs bridge+glasses), at 240 fps or higher; resolution is one capture frame (4.2 ms at 240 fps) before sub-frame interpolation. Rolling-shutter phones read rows top to bottom, so keep the screen regions at the same height in the image and film the glasses' eyepiece through its optics (general camera practice, not sourced here). To isolate the bridge, compare "FPGA -> bridge -> glasses" with "PC flashing square -> glasses over native USB-C DP" using the same LED procedure.
- A Raspberry Pi or PC is the cheapest arbitrary-timing generator; confirm KMS support before relying on legacy `hdmi_timings`. Dedicated generators (Quantum Data, Unigraf, Murideo) were not researched.

### Gaps
- No verified cheap HDMI signal generator/tester with user-set timings; Raspberry Pi KMS behaviour for custom timings unconfirmed.
- AW EDID Editor not verified; hardware EDID sniffers/readers (I2C) not researched.
- Whether the Tang Nano 20K HDMI connector exposes DDC/HPD for reading the sink's EDID: unverified.

## 6. CVBS-to-HDMI "AV2HDMI" dongles (MS1858 / MacroSilicon, latency) as a comparison path from analog goggles' AV out

### Takeaway
The one chip datasheet I could verify, MacroSilicon MS1858E, is a single-chip CVBS/S-Video to HDMI-style (TMDS) converter with 3D comb decoding, 3D deinterlacing, scaling, frame-rate conversion and an internal storage unit, whose strap pins select only 720p60 or 1080p60 output; it publishes no latency figure. No measured latency for any AV2HDMI dongle was found, and retail statements range from vague to anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR] MacroSilicon MS1858E datasheet (doc HJ-BMJL-PD-002 rev B/0, 19 pages, in Chinese, marked confidential): "single chip" conversion of analog CVBS and S-Video to "HD" output (the text never says HDMI or DVI; the HD pins are a TMDS core with DDC and HPD); video input stage listed as two 9-bit ADCs at 54 MHz (the ADC parameter table gives 8-bit resolution), 0.45-1.1 Vpp input; built-in 3D video decoding, OSD and a "storage unit" (the block diagram shows an SDRAM block with a dynamic memory controller, TVD decoder, DEINT deinterlacer, video processor and TMDS core); 3D deinterlacing, horizontal/vertical scaling; "supports frame-rate conversion and interlaced-to-progressive conversion"; output 24-bit RGB/YUV up to WUXGA with DDC, HPD and A/V mute; pin GPIO1 low selects 720P60 output and GPIO1 high 1080P60, GPIO0 selects CVBS vs S-Video (GPIO functions can be redefined through an external EEPROM; master mode uses an internal MCU with firmware, slave mode I2C control); HD transmitter clock range 25-148.5 MHz; supplies 3.3 V (typ. 85 mA) and 1.2 V (typ. 140 mA); QFN48 7x7 mm; 27 MHz crystal; applications "high-quality set-top box, security surveillance". The datasheet text contains no latency figure and no mention of PAL, NTSC or 50 Hz.
- (2nd-hand, retailer listings, links omitted) Cheap AV-to-HDMI boxes advertise PAL/NTSC composite input and "HDMI output: 1080p/60Hz, 720p/60Hz"; one listing warns of possible lag "less than three second", a buyer estimated "probably like half a second"; none names the chip or gives a measured figure.
- (2nd-hand; low-quality, affiliate-style page) A retro-gaming roundup says sub-USD-15 RCA-to-HDMI adapters "introduce noticeable input delay and blurry output" without numbers. — [narcosis-the-game.com roundup (Sep 2026)](https://www.narcosis-the-game.com/best-retro-console-av-to-hdmi-converters/)
- [USER] IntoFPV thread (Jun 2024): one poster "would expect much latency by hdmi to a tv monitor"; another says the goggles' HDMI interface needs the right USB-C to HDMI cable and that a Walksnail VRX "can connect via HDMI fairly easily"; no AV-to-HDMI converter brand, chip, latency or PAL/50 Hz data is given. — [IntoFPV thread 26362](https://intofpv.com/archive/index.php/thread-26362.html)
- No source in this session ties the generic "AV2HDMI" dongles to the MS1858 specifically (the brief's chipset suggestion); no teardown was found.

### Inferences
- A PAL source through an MS1858-class strap-configured dongle would come out at 60 Hz (720p60 or 1080p60), which means frame-rate conversion with buffering; so this comparison path cannot test 50 Hz sink acceptance and would add at least the deinterlacer/frame-rate-converter delay (3D deinterlacing alone needs several fields, so a delay of the order of a few frames, tens of ms, is plausible; no number found). Measure it with the same LED/high-speed method as a labelled stage.
- Useful comparison design: analog goggles AV out (CVBS) -> AV2HDMI -> same bridge -> same glasses, versus the FPGA path; keep the dongle's HDMI mode fixed (720p60) so sink acceptance is not a variable.

### Gaps
- No measured latency (ms or frames) for MS1858E-based or other AV2HDMI dongles; no frame-rate behaviour with 50 Hz CVBS input (the datasheet is silent; the strap pins offer only 60 Hz outputs).
- The MS1858E datasheet is confidential-marked and Chinese; other MacroSilicon CVBS-to-HDMI parts (MS1861 and similar) were not checked.
- No evidence which chips real "AV2HDMI" dongles contain.
